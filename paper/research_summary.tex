{\Large\bfseries What Bars Cannot See: Order Flow, Its OHLCV Proxies, and Short-Horizon Bitcoin Returns}\\[3pt]
Seyed Abolfazl Hosseini, Ferdowsi University of Mashhad\\
Research summary, November 2026 \hfill Paper and code: \href{https://github.com/a0x0h/what-bars-cannot-see}{github.com/a0x0h/what-bars-cannot-see}

\vspace{4pt}\hrule\vspace{6pt}

\textbf{Question.} Bulk volume classification (BVC), Kyle's lambda and high-low spread estimators are
used to study order flow and liquidity when trade-level data are unavailable, and increasingly as
features in return forecasts. How much of the true quantity do they capture, and does the true
quantity or its proxy predict short-horizon returns out of sample and after costs?

\textbf{Data and identification.} Binance spot BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT, January 2022 to October 2026,
hourly and one-minute bars (about 42{,}000 hours and 2.5 million minutes per asset). The exchange
reports the taker-buy volume of every bar, so the true signed order flow is observed; on one month of
trade-level data the bar-level imbalance matches trade-level aggressor flow with correlation
\aggCorr. A zero-fee promotion on BTC/USDT only (July 2022 to March 2023) gives a natural comparison
with ETH/USDT.

\textbf{Methods.} (1) Proxy validation against observed flow and one-minute realised volatility, and
a nonparametric \emph{information ceiling}: the $R^2$ of the best possible function of the bar's
standardised return, an upper bound for every return-based classifier including BVC and the tick
rule. (2) Return response to one-SD flow shocks over 1--24 hours, separating the part of flow visible
in the bar's price from the part that is not (Newey-West inference). (3) Monthly walk-forward over
\BTCnFoldsWF{} test months with purged labels and all preprocessing and tuning inside each training
window; elastic net and gradient boosting on nested feature sets; Clark-West, Diebold-Mariano and
block-bootstrap inference; overlapping-sleeve trading rules with costs and the deflated Sharpe ratio.
Automated tests verify that no feature uses future data.

\textbf{Findings.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \emph{Volatility is measured well, order flow is not.} Garman-Klass volatility from hourly bars
has correlation \BTCgkCorr{} with realised volatility. BVC imbalance has correlation \BTCbvcCorr{} with
observed imbalance, and no function of the bar return explains more than \BTCceilIS{} of its variance
even in sample (\BTCceilISm{} on one-minute bars): most aggressor flow leaves no trace in the bar's
price, BVC is already near this bound, and its scale drifts over time. Kyle's
lambda from BVC has correlation \BTClambdaCorr{} with lambda from true flow. The Corwin-Schultz spread
has a median of \BTCcsMedian{} bps on a market whose tick is \BTCtick{} bps and moves with volatility
(correlation \BTCcsVolCorr). Results are nearly identical for ETH.
\item \emph{In BTC, the information in flow is in the part bars cannot see.} A one-SD taker-imbalance
shock is followed by a price decline of \BTCfrTakAtwo{} bps over two hours ($t=\BTCfrTakTtwo$), robust to
lagged returns, volume and volatility and stable across fee regimes and sample halves. Holding the bar
return fixed leaves it unchanged. Predictive content rises with measurement accuracy: none for hourly
BVC, part for BVC on one-minute bars, all for observed flow, which subsumes the proxy. In ETH the
effect is weaker and not significant.
\item \emph{Detectable out of sample only when measured, and too small to trade.} Used alone in a
monthly walk-forward, observed imbalance forecasts BTC returns out of sample ($R^2_{OOS}=\BTCspRtwo$\%,
Clark-West $p=\BTCspPtwo$ at two hours, significant in both halves); hourly BVC does not. Traded on
its own, the signal breaks even at about \BTCstBEselective{} bp per unit of turnover. No multivariate model using
microstructure proxies, observed flow or realised measures beats a zero forecast; cross-validation
selects the empty model in most months. The best of \BTCnTrials{} trading rules has a gross Sharpe ratio of
\BTCbestSR{} but a deflated Sharpe ratio of \BTCbestDSR, and no rule breaks even at the standard fee.
\item \emph{Fees change the data.} Binance's zero-fee promotion on BTC/USDT multiplied hourly trade
counts fivefold and cut the dispersion of aggressor imbalance from \BTCsdImbStd{} to \BTCsdImbZero;
ETH/USDT, not included, was unaffected.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why it matters.} Bar-based flow and spread measures should not be read as measures of order
flow or transaction costs in liquid electronic markets, and the predictive content of order flow
at intraday horizons is precisely what such proxies discard. The natural next steps are order-book
data at higher frequency, where flow imbalance at the best quotes is known to matter, and flow
measured across venues.
